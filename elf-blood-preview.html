CHAPTER 1

The Life Before

The mansion · Present

Before sunrise, Taylor found an oak with one branch bent low across the path. Its leaves had curled inward, and yesterday’s rain still filled a split in the bark. She set a bare foot between two roots and rested her palm against the trunk. The cold wood made her fingers ache. Beyond the trees, the mansion’s kitchen window glowed yellow. Reyla was already inside, moving dishes quietly so she would not wake the house.

Taylor liked this hour because nothing expected an explanation from her. Mist gathered in the garden around flowers too tall for their beds. Two winged creatures chased each other over the stream, calling indignantly. She smiled at the damaged branch. “You are not doing very well,” she told the oak.

The leaves stayed curled. Taylor moved her palm along the trunk, searching for a change in the cold. She often knew where to touch a living thing before she understood what was wrong with it. Trying to explain that knowledge only made it harder to use. “You could at least tell me,” she murmured.

“It is a tree,” Breni said from the path.

Taylor turned. Breni stood where the mist thinned, tall enough for the low branch to brush her shoulder. Dark leather covered her armour, and the silver hilts of her daggers showed at her waist. Her blue-green hair had escaped its fastening overnight. A loose strand settled against her cheek, and Taylor felt the familiar pleasure of finding her there.

“I had noticed.”

Breni looked at the hand on the bark. “Then perhaps you can forgive its manners.”

Taylor laughed and faced the tree again. Green light spread between her fingers and traced the ridges in the bark. A resistance gave way beneath her hand. The injured branch lifted, its leaves uncurling one by one. The damp scent of the wood grew sharp enough to taste. When the light faded, Taylor stared at the healed split, unsettled by how naturally she had done something she could not explain.

Breni came beside her. “You have been doing that more often.”

“Helping trees?”

“Not knowing what you can do.”

Taylor wiped her palm on her trousers. She could still feel the oak alive beneath its bark. Healing a branch ought to have been a small kindness, but lately every such act hinted at something larger. “Does it worry you?” she asked.

Breni examined the repaired split without reaching for it. “It worries me that it happens when you are alone.”

Taylor leaned her shoulder against her wife's arm. The leather smelled of smoke and the oil Breni used on her weapons. She knew the scent better than she knew the first day of her own life. Her memories began beside water, with Breni looking down at her and asking questions she could not answer. Years had supplied many answers since then, though none had reached behind that stream. “You cannot stand beside every tree I visit.”

“I can try.”

Taylor tilted her head to look up at her. “That sounded like a threat to the trees.”

Breni's mouth softened. She brushed a damp strand of golden hair away from Taylor's face, careful of the piercings along her long ear. Tiny colours caught among the strands, the rainbow stars Taylor had never managed to explain any better than the green light. Breni touched them without hesitation. They were part of the woman she had loved for decades, and that familiarity made Taylor grateful in a way she could not put into a joke.

As they walked back toward the house, Taylor glanced over her shoulder once. The branch held itself above the path, restored, and the forest looked unchanged. She wished the feeling inside her could have been restored as easily.
